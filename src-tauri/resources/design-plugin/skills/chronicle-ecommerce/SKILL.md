# Shopping-page rules

Twelve rules for product, category, cart and checkout frames. Each has a one-line reason and the public article to read for the research behind it. These are our own words; read the articles, do not paste from them.

1. **Price and per-unit price are visible above the fold on every breakpoint.** Shoppers who must hunt for the price assume the worst. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/
2. **Delivery cost and date sit beside the primary action.** Unexpected shipping cost is a leading reason people abandon a cart. https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability-report-and-benchmark
3. **The returns policy is named on the page, not only linked from the footer.** It removes the last risk before the click. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/
4. **One primary action is above the fold on the narrowest frame.** On a phone the add-to-cart button must not be the thing that scrolled away. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-ux/
5. **Product images offer a zoom or gallery with real alternates (angles, in use, scale).** Images stand in for touching the product. https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-product-image-gallery
6. **Size and variant pickers show stock and never hide the chosen value.** Picking an unavailable option and finding out late is a trust loss. https://baymard.com/blog/product-page-ux-variations
7. **Reviews show a count and a distribution, never a bare star.** A lone average is easy to doubt; the spread is what people read. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/
8. **Gift options are named where a gift buyer looks (gift wrap, message, ship-to-other).** Gift buyers decide on whether it can be a gift. https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability-report-and-benchmark
9. **A sticky add-to-cart appears on phones once the first one scrolls away.** The action should stay one thumb away for the whole page. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-ux/
10. **No carousel for the primary information.** Carousels hide content that most visitors never advance to. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/
11. **Trust signals sit near the payment fields, not in a footer.** Doubt peaks where the card number goes in. https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form
12. **Checkout shows its progress and offers guest checkout.** Forced accounts and unknown length both cost completions. https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability-report-and-benchmark

Baymard's public index of UX research articles is https://baymard.com/blog and NN/g's e-commerce collection is https://www.nngroup.com/topic/e-commerce/. When a rule seems to conflict with the brief, follow the brief and say so in the findings.
